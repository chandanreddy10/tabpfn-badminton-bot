How to play

Singles badminton seen from above. You are the first player, starting in the bottom half.

Move

WASD moves you. In two-player mode, the second player uses the ←↑↓→ arrow keys.

Serve

Click a spot inside the shaded diagonal service box. You serve from the right court on an even score and from the left on an odd score.

Return the shuttle

Get the shuttle's landing spot inside your dashed reach ring before it comes down. You then have 2 seconds to choose a shot and click where to send it.

Shots

1 Clear: high and deep. 2 Drop: short, lands near the net. 3 Smash: fast but less accurate. The faint circle at your cursor shows where the shot may land; wind adds a drift you can't see.

Scoring

Every rally scores a point. A shot that lands out, a serve outside the box, or a shot nobody reaches gives the point to the other side. First to 21, win by 2, capped at 30. The rally winner serves next.

Your opponent: TabPFN-3.5

It warms up briefly before your first match and keeps learning while you play. Its shots fly slower to give you time to react. The game slows down for a moment while TabPFN-3.5 is predicting.

Press H any time to see this again (the game pauses), Esc to close.

How to play
  • Move with WASD (in two-player mode, the second player uses the arrow keys).
  • To serve, click a spot inside the shaded diagonal service box.
  • Get the landing spot inside your dashed reach ring, then choose a shot (1 2 3) and click a target within 2 seconds.
  • Press H at any time for the full instructions.
What TabPFN-3.5 sees and how it decides
  • Observation (every tick): own position, velocity and previous command; opponent position and velocity; phase; whether it must hit; valid zones; score; reach, speed limit, court size, nominal shot timing.
  • During a flight: shot type cue, time since the hit, noisy shuttle samples every 50 ms. After landing: where it landed and the flight time. Never wind, noise, or the landing before it lands.
  • Landing (TabPFN): at 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 s after the opponent's hit, a row of [time since hit, observed x/y/z at each checkpoint so far, velocity, shot cue, hitter and bot position] predicts landing minus the last observed position, and the remaining flight time, with 10/50/90 % quantiles.
  • Movement (TabPFN): rows [x, y, vx, vy, ux, uy, previous ux, uy] predict the velocity change per 0.1 s. One batched 625-row grid query gives a piecewise-linear surrogate (tagged tabpfn-linearized).
  • Planner: CEM over 1.2 s of commands, 8 Monte Carlo rollouts per candidate with TabPFN's uncertainty, and a chance constraint P(reach) ≥ 90 %.
  • Own shots (TabPFN): random candidate targets scored by TabPFN's predicted landing spread and the opponent's distance.
